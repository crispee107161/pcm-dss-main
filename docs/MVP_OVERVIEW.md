# PC Merchandise DSS — What the MVP Employs

**Date:** 2026-08-12 (updated same day — FR-25/FR-27/FR-30 revisions folded in; see §7, §10, and §11)
**Sources:** `docs/CHAPTER 1_Capstone.txt` and `docs/PCM_DSS_Developer_Handoff - New.md`, cross-checked against `docs/data_catalog.md` and `docs/mvp.md`, and against the raw files in `data/New_FB_Ads_Data/`, `data/FB_OrganicPosts_Data/`, and `data/FB_PageLevel_Data/`.

This document is a narrative companion to `data_catalog.md` (data reference) and `mvp.md` (scope reference). Where those two answer "what is the exact value of X," this one answers "what is this system, and why is it shaped this way" — intended to be read start to finish by an adviser, a panelist, or a new group member without cross-referencing anything else.

## 1. Purpose and the problem it addresses

PC Merchandise is a computer hardware retailer in San Jose del Monte, Bulacan, employing ten staff and running paid Facebook campaigns continuously since 2022, with roughly 80 percent of customer acquisition attributed to the platform. The business holds twelve months of advertising and content records containing every measure needed to compare performance — and yet its marketing decisions rest on view counts, the owner's recollection, and verbally collected attribution that is never written down. The gap is not one of data availability but of processing method, which is precisely the class of problem Gorry and Scott Morton (1971) called semi-structured: data and analysis apply, judgment remains necessary, and the system's job is to supply the former while leaving the latter with the decision-maker.

Chapter 1 identifies six specific conditions the system answers. Performance information sits in three separate exports that are never brought together and share no common identifier. The cost of generating a customer inquiry varies from ₱8.05 to ₱66.61 across advertisements while the reporting the business consults never surfaces that spread. Content promotion is governed by a single unvalidated volume measure — view count — applied uniformly across content types including entertainment content explicitly designed to accumulate views. Budget decisions are made from memory, with the owner estimating ₱100,000 monthly against recorded expenditure ranging ₱45,600 to ₱76,100. Customer attribution is collected verbally and discarded. And content carries no category classification at all, so performance cannot be compared by content type at the moment promotion decisions are made.

The MVP therefore consolidates the three exports into one repository, classifies organic content into the four categories the client already uses, and reports **efficiency rather than volume** — cost per inquiry for advertising, engagement rate for organic content. It is explicitly not a system that predicts inquiries from spend; the predictive layer is cut, for reasons given in section 9.

## 2. The data foundation

The system is built on a single authoritative export set covering 1 August 2025 to 31 July 2026. Every structural figure below was independently re-derived from the raw files rather than taken on trust from the specification, and the specification proved accurate.

The **advertising export** is twelve monthly files sharing 93 identical columns across 746 rows, UTF-8 with BOM, header on row one. Its grain is one row per advertisement per reporting month, and the pair `(Ad ID, Reporting starts)` is genuinely unique — 746 rows produce 746 distinct pairs. The hierarchy runs 26 campaigns to 26 ad set IDs to 309 ad IDs, and this is where the first trap lives: those 309 ad IDs carry only 297 distinct ad names, and those 26 ad set IDs carry only 24 distinct ad set names. Names are reused. Every aggregation in the system must therefore key on identifiers and treat names purely as display labels, or two genuinely distinct ad sets will silently merge into one row. Fifteen of the 93 columns are entirely null and must not be required by the validator. Total spend reconciles to ₱901,196.96, of which ₱740,382.55 belongs to the 486 rows optimised for messaging conversations.

The **organic post export** is twelve monthly files totalling 730 rows and 730 unique post IDs, again UTF-8 with BOM. Its column count varies — 32, 33, or 34 depending on the month — but only the two negative-feedback columns move; every measurement column is stable. This instability is the reason type detection must match a required subset of columns rather than an exact tuple, since equality matching would reject seven of the twelve valid files. The export's grain is one row per post carrying lifetime cumulative totals, which matters enormously: re-exporting the same month at a later date yields different numbers, verified as differing on every one of 81 September posts. The system must use this one export set and never merge in files pulled on another date.

The **page-level export** nominally contains nine files, but only six are the 365-row daily series the specification describes — Follows, Interactions, Link clicks, Viewers, Views, and Visits, each covering 2025-08-01 through 2026-07-31 with no gaps and no blank values. These are UTF-16 LE with a two-line preamble before the real header, where line one is an Excel separator hint and line two is the metric name used to route values into the correct column. The remaining three files are demographic snapshots of a different shape, and one of them, `Audience.csv`, is not a single table at all but four stacked blocks whose final block is a 365-row Follows series duplicating `Follows (1).csv` outright.

Overarching all of this is a structural constraint that shapes the schema: **there is no key linking an organic post to the advertisement it became.** Ad IDs and post IDs occupy different namespaces, every one of the 93 advertising columns was scanned against all 730 post IDs with zero matches, and fuzzy matching ad names against captions tops out at a median best score of 0.45. The consequence is that no foreign key may exist between the posts and ads tables, that FR-21's correlation runs entirely within advertising data while FR-19's ranking comparison runs entirely within organic data, and that the only legitimate cross-source link is the calendar month.

## 3. Ingestion

The ingestion pipeline reads the first bytes of an uploaded file to determine encoding, treating a UTF-16 byte-order mark as the page-level family and everything else as UTF-8 with BOM stripped. If the first line is the literal `sep=,` the file is a page-level daily series whose header sits on line three and whose metric identity comes from line two. Otherwise the header row is parsed and matched against required column subsets: the presence of `Ad ID` alongside `Amount spent (PHP)` identifies an advertising export, `Post ID` alongside `Reactions, Comments and Shares` identifies an organic export, and anything matching neither is rejected with the header list shown to the user.

Cleaning happens in a fixed order that matters. The BOM is stripped and the file decoded, header whitespace is trimmed because Meta occasionally pads it, numerics are parsed with thousands separators removed and blanks becoming null rather than zero, dates are parsed, all text is NFKC-normalised, records are deduplicated on their natural keys, and only then are derived measures computed. Those natural keys are `(Ad ID, Reporting starts)` for advertising, `Post ID` for organic posts, `(metric, Date)` for page-level series, and the label value for demographics. Re-uploading a month must update rather than duplicate, and on the organic side an update must **overwrite** measure values rather than add to them, since those values are cumulative and adding would double-count.

Every upload returns an ingestion summary reporting rows read, stored, updated, rejected with reasons, and skipped as duplicates. This is how the team reconciles system state against source files, and the specification is explicit that it is a graded requirement rather than a nicety.

## 4. Derived measures

Three formulas carry the analytical weight, and ambiguity in any of them produces numbers that disagree with the written chapters.

Organic engagement rate is `Reactions, Comments and Shares` divided by `Reach`, computable for all 730 posts with a verified median of 0.0069. Advertising engagement rate is `Post engagements` divided by `Reach`, and the aggregate `Post engagements` column must be used rather than summing reactions, comments, and shares — Meta suppresses low counts in the component columns, which are populated on only 670, 360, and 388 rows respectively against 739 for the aggregate, so summing components would silently understate engagement on roughly half the data. Cost per inquiry is total spend divided by total results, computed only for advertisements whose result type is "Messaging conversations started", left null rather than zero for every other objective, and excluding the 36 rows carrying a blank result type entirely.

Underlying all three is a single rule that the specification calls the most common source of wrong numbers: **sum, then divide.** Cost per inquiry for any group is the sum of spend over the sum of results, never the average of per-row cost-per-result values, because averaging weights a ₱50 advertisement equally with a ₱50,000 one. Since 211 of 309 advertisements span multiple months, this error would surface at every level of aggregation — advertisement, ad set, campaign, category, and month. The same rule governs engagement rate. Where a denominator sums to zero the result is null and renders as an em dash, never as zero or infinity.

## 5. Content categorisation

Organic posts are classified into the four categories in use at the business — product showcase, promotional offer, testimonial, and entertainment — which correspond to Dolan et al.'s (2019) informational, remunerative, relational, and entertaining framework. Because content type is recorded in no structured field, classification must be inferred from the caption text, and two properties of that text will silently destroy the feature if handled carelessly. First, the caption lives in either `Title` or `Description` depending on post type, with `Title` populated on 725 of 730 rows and `Description` on only 379, so the system must take whichever field is longer rather than reading one column. Second, 177 of the 730 captions use stylised Unicode — a post reading in mathematical-bold characters will not match a plain keyword — so NFKC normalisation must run before any keyword rule, any LLM call, and any storage, with both the raw and normalised forms retained.

Two suggestion methods compete. The keyword method scores captions against a weighted category lexicon with a deterministic tie-break, returning `UNCLASSIFIED` on a zero score rather than forcing a guess. Its expected performance is modest — a probe over all 730 captions left 139 matching no category and 175 matching more than one, roughly 43 percent ambiguous — and the specification is emphatic that the lexicon must not be tuned until it beats the LLM, because that would invalidate the comparison the study exists to make. The LLM method classifies each post in batches at temperature zero for reproducibility, returning structured JSON, retrying once on a parse failure and then marking the post unclassified, with the raw response, model name, and run date persisted. Categorisation runs asynchronously to upload and an LLM failure never blocks ingestion.

The comparison itself, FR-15, imposes a hard schema requirement: three separate permanent columns per post — the keyword suggestion, the LLM suggestion, and the marketing manager's final assignment. The system reports percentage agreement and Cohen's kappa for each method against the final label, treating `UNCLASSIFIED` as a fifth label in the confusion matrix so that a method cannot score well by abstaining, and reporting n alongside every kappa. If this comparison were implemented as a throwaway script rather than as database columns, the requirement is not satisfied.

## 6. The analysis layer

Four requirements examine whether the business's current practice is sound, and they run inside the system against whatever data is in the repository rather than as one-off study scripts.

FR-19 tests the promotion practice directly by ranking posts by view count and by engagement rate, reporting the Spearman correlation between the two rankings and the overlap between the top 10 and top 20 percent under each measure. The single post with a blank view count is excluded explicitly, since a null sorted to either end would corrupt both the correlation and the overlap. FR-20 reports the distribution of views and engagement rate across the four categories with n displayed per category, so a sparse category cannot be read as a solid comparison. FR-21 correlates advertising engagement rate against cost per inquiry across the 187 advertisements with computable cost per inquiry — and here the implementation order is itself a graded requirement. A Shapiro-Wilk normality test runs on both variables first, the outcome of that test **selects** Pearson or Spearman, and only the selected coefficient is displayed. Computing both and showing the more favourable one is a defect a panelist finds by reading the code. On the current data cost per inquiry has a skew of roughly 3.9 so Spearman will be chosen, but the system must decide that at runtime rather than assume it.

FR-22 governs how all of this is presented. Every analytical result displays its coefficient, its n, and its significance together, with the plain-language interpretation keyed to magnitude rather than to the p-value — because across 730 posts a correlation of 0.08 reaches statistical significance while explaining under one percent of variance, and a UI that prints "significant" alone will be over-read.

## 7. The features that make it actionable

Six additional requirements were added after a full re-analysis of the client files, chosen because each is derivable from data already in hand, actionable by a named user, and absent from the original requirement list. Every figure below reproduces exactly from the raw data.

**Budget reallocation (FR-25)** is the highest-value feature and is built first — but its first version had a real flaw, caught and corrected the same day it was specified. Ranking all 187 messaging advertisements by cost per inquiry into unfiltered quartiles looked convincing at first, but the worst quartile turned out to be defined by *volume*, not quality: its median spend was ₱333 against the best quartile's ₱5,587, and an advertisement with eight inquiries produces an extremely noisy cost-per-inquiry figure by construction — a case of regression to the mean, not genuine inefficiency. The corrected version filters to advertisements spending at least ₱1,000 before quartiling (n=108, threshold provisional pending Chapter 1):

| Quartile | Ads | Spend | Inquiries | CPI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 (best) | 27 | ₱375,809.90 | 31,875 | ₱11.79 |
| Q2 | 27 | ₱170,739.45 | 10,360 | ₱16.48 |
| Q3 | 27 | ₱104,309.29 | 5,116 | ₱20.39 |
| Q4 (worst) | 27 | ₱59,745.30 | 1,988 | ₱30.05 |

The corrected headline is larger and more defensible than the original: the worst quartile spent ₱59,745 to generate 1,988 inquiries, whereas the same money at the best quartile's rate would have produced approximately 5,067 — about 3,079 additional inquiries for zero additional spend. The screen carries a mandatory caption stating that this is a retrospective comparison of recorded results and that past efficiency does not guarantee the same rate at higher spend, and it must never be labelled a forecast, projection, or simulation.

**Ad set and campaign ranking (FR-26)** groups messaging advertisements by identifier, reporting spend, inquiries, cost per inquiry, and ad count sorted ascending, flagging any group with fewer than three advertisements as low-confidence. The spread between best and worst ad set is roughly 2.2×, and because the owner named these ad sets himself the comparison needs no interpretation layer — though the screen should note that different ad sets ran in different periods with different targeting, so this compares what happened rather than a controlled test.

**Lifecycle diagnostics (FR-27)** produces a finding that contradicts conventional advice — cost per inquiry improves with advertisement age — but, like FR-25, its first version needed a correction before the finding could be trusted. A raw curve of cost per inquiry by week of life is contaminated by survivorship bias: it mixes advertisements that were killed off early with ones that ran long, so an apparent improvement with age could simply be poor advertisements leaving the denominator rather than any advertisement actually improving over its own lifetime. Restricting to a cohort that survived to a fixed horizon confirmed the finding was real, not an artefact — but that weekly-resolution check depended on a 19-column daily export that is not present anywhere in this repository, so its specific figures (a cohort of 44 advertisements surviving eleven weeks, cost per inquiry falling from ₱14.63 to ₱12.57) remain unverified from anything currently available.

The system does not need those figures to ship the feature. FR-27 is rebuilt entirely on **month-of-life**, using only the monthly export the system already ingests, keyed correctly on advertisement ID rather than the weaker advertisement-name key the daily file would have required. Restricting to the 114 advertisements that survived at least two months, cost per inquiry falls from ₱15.53 in month zero to ₱13.03 by month three; restricting further to the 50 advertisements surviving at least three months, it falls from ₱14.66 to ₱13.03 over the same span. Advertisements running a single month average ₱14.66 against ₱13.58 for those spanning four or more calendar months — the same direction as the original weekly finding, reproduced independently against data that actually exists, and requiring no additional export from the client. Frequency continues to correlate negatively with cost per inquiry at ρ = −0.241, and no ad fatigue is detectable at this account's frequency levels, which means retiring advertisements early is more likely costing inquiries than saving them.

**Watch-through rate (FR-28)** divides average seconds viewed by duration, computable for 397 posts, and matters because it correlates with engagement rate at ρ = 0.363 — substantially stronger than view count does. This strengthens the study's fourth objective considerably: rather than only criticising the client's view-count practice, the system offers a demonstrably better alternative drawn from the same export.

**Post type comparison (FR-29)** shows a real trade-off rather than a ranking. Reels achieve nearly twice the engagement rate of photos (0.0108 against 0.0059) but the lowest median reach (1,086 against 1,884 for videos), and with only 70 reels against 331 photos and 328 videos, the n must be visible on every row — and because Meta distributes reels differently from photos algorithmically, the comparison reflects both content quality and platform distribution, not content quality alone.

**Follows per 100 page visits (FR-30)**, renamed from an earlier "page growth funnel" framing, closes the loop on the page itself: 389,577 visits against 11,386 new follows over twelve months, or 2.92 follows per 100 visits, with monthly follows ranging 652 to 1,641. The rename matters — visits and follows are two independently collected daily series with no per-user link between them, so calling the ratio a funnel or a conversion rate implies an attribution the data cannot actually support.

## 8. Roles and access

The system has exactly three roles. The **Owner** makes advertising and budget decisions and holds full access, including exclusive control of user management. The **Marketing Manager** heads the marketing team and holds exclusive authority over final category assignment, which is the pivot on which the entire FR-15 comparison turns. **Marketing Team Members** produce content and are largely view-only, though FR-28 and FR-29 were added specifically to give them something concrete to act on. The admin manager who handles Facebook messages is deliberately not a system user, since the system ingests CSV exports rather than live messages and offers that person no task; any figures they need arrive through exported reports.

Access is enforced by two rules. Screens a role cannot use are **hidden entirely from the sidebar rather than disabled**, because greyed-out items invite confusion during the technology acceptance evaluation and depress ease-of-use scores. And hiding a sidebar link is not access control — the role must be checked server-side on every request. After login the Owner lands on the dashboard, the Marketing Manager lands on the categorisation queue if it is non-empty and the dashboard otherwise, and everyone else lands on the dashboard.

The eleven screens are the dashboard, upload, content library, categorisation review, advertising performance, content performance, analysis, method evaluation, reports, user management, and audit log. The dashboard answers "how are we doing" and deliberately excludes the analysis results, which answer "is our method sound" — mixing them buries both.

## 9. What the MVP deliberately does not do

Five previously planned analytics features are cut, and because none appears in the Objectives of the Study, none needs to be defended, retracted, or written up as a limitation.

**What-if and Monte Carlo simulation** is cut because a simulation is only as good as the model it samples from, and ours would rest on a regression explaining roughly 40 to 47 percent of variance in a small observational sample. More fundamentally, the advertising data is observational rather than experimental, so the coefficients describe associations among advertisements that ran, not what would happen if a setting were changed — a slider implying a causal lever the data cannot support has no good answer when a panelist asks how we know changing X causes Y. **Holt-Winters forecasting** is cut because seasonal decomposition requires at least two complete cycles and twelve monthly observations provide exactly one; daily forecasting is technically possible but the client authorises budget monthly, so a forecast of tomorrow's spend answers no question he actually asks. FR-18's month-over-month comparison replaces it, being descriptive, assumption-free, and aimed squarely at the real problem of budget decided from memory.

**Multiple linear regression** is the interesting cut, because it genuinely works — regressing log cost per inquiry on advertisement characteristics gives R² = 0.465 at n = 187 with several significant predictors. It is cut anyway because it is unclaimed scope, because reach and spend are near-perfectly collinear at a VIF around 500 (bigger budgets simply buy more reach) with even a reduced specification carrying VIF of 12 to 19, and because FR-21 already answers the same underlying question with far less methodological exposure. If an adviser later requests a predictive component, this is the one to bring back — not simulation or forecasting — with a documented variable-selection step written into Chapter 3 first. **Lagged correlation** is cut because there is no organic-to-ads join key and therefore nothing to lag, and the **campaign health score** is cut because its composite weights would be arbitrary when FR-25 and FR-26 give the same answer from raw figures.

Seven further ideas were investigated and rejected, recorded so nobody re-investigates them: day-of-week posting shows engagement flat across all seven days; hour-of-day is confounded because 367 of 730 posts publish between midnight and 6am, almost certainly scheduling rather than audience behaviour; Meta's quality and engagement-rate rankings are blank on 697 of 746 rows; negative feedback has ten non-zero rows across twelve months; content-category-to-ad-efficiency is permanently blocked by the missing join key; ROAS and revenue metrics have no underlying data since Messenger inquiries are the terminal recorded event; and the organic-versus-boosted split is not available as a Meta export option.

## 10. Where the current codebase diverges

The existing implementation was built for a materially different system, and the gaps are structural rather than cosmetic. The role enum carries `SALES_DIRECTOR`, a role that does not exist in this specification, which places the entire sales dashboard tree and its report branch out of scope. The `Ad` model is keyed on the composite of ad name, ad set name, and reporting start, and carries no ad ID, ad set ID, campaign ID, campaign name, post engagements, frequency, views, or viewers columns at all — this directly contradicts the rule against keying on names and is the first blocker before any ingestion work can proceed. The `FacebookPost` model carries a single nullable category foreign key where FR-15 requires three permanent columns, and lacks the duration and average-seconds-viewed fields FR-28 needs.

On the logic side, keyword detection currently returns the first substring match with no scoring, no tie-break, no unclassified outcome, and no NFKC normalisation anywhere in the path. Groq is called to suggest keywords for the lexicon rather than to classify posts, so the LLM classification method is entirely unbuilt. Neither Cohen's kappa nor Shapiro-Wilk exists anywhere in the repository, and Spearman is currently computed unconditionally, meaning the method-selection ordering that FR-21 grades has no implementation. The CSV detector's advertising signature keys on columns that the current 93-column export does not carry in the expected form, the demographics signature fails on a capitalisation mismatch, and the page-metric name map has no entry for `Viewers`, so that file throws on upload today. The codebase also still carries a distinct `ADS_DAILY_CSV` type built for the 19-column daily export — since the system has since decided to ingest the monthly export only (section 7), that detection branch and its validator should be retired rather than adapted.

Finally, five statistics modules back cut features and remain in place with passing tests. The specification's guidance is to leave them out of the build rather than rework them; whether to eventually delete them is a housekeeping decision for the team, not a blocker.

## 11. Build order and verification gate

The sequence is deliberate. Authentication and the three roles come first because everything needs a user context. Ingestion follows, since nothing works without data. Third comes the repository and derived measures — and this step carries a **hard stop**: totals must reconcile to ₱901,196.96 across all advertisements and ₱740,382.55 for messaging-optimised ones before any further work proceeds, because every downstream figure inherits an error introduced here. The content library and manual categorisation path come fourth, the dashboard with the follows-per-100-visits figure fifth.

Budget reallocation and ad set ranking are promoted to sixth and seventh, ahead of where a conventional ordering would put them, because they are the two features that answer "where is the money going" in a form the owner can act on immediately, and both are cheap group-by aggregations over data already ingested. Performance screens follow, then the categorisation algorithms, method evaluation, the analysis screen, lifecycle diagnostics, and finally reports and the audit log. The first several steps produce a working system; the categorisation and analysis steps are what make it a decision support system rather than a dashboard, and they are the objectives the panel will ask about, so they must not slip to the final week.

## 12. Evaluation

Two frameworks assess the finished system. ISO/IEC 25010 covers functional suitability, performance efficiency, compatibility, usability, reliability, security, maintainability, and portability, and is normally administered to IT professionals in addition to intended users, so its respondent pool is not constrained. The Technology Acceptance Model measures perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, and behavioural intention among PC Merchandise's marketing personnel and management — and because the system has three roles, that pool is small. If the total falls under roughly five respondents, the count must be stated explicitly in Chapter 3 rather than reporting means as though drawn from a larger sample. A small n honestly reported is defensible; a mean of three responses presented without its n is not.

## 13. Decisions still owned by the team

Six items remain open and are not the developer's to settle. The minimum-spend threshold for the cost-per-inquiry population must be stated in Chapter 1 with the system default matching it — FR-25's quartile filter currently defaults to ₱1,000 provisionally, pending that decision. The Views-versus-Viewers ambiguity from the interview needs one message to the owner to confirm which measure he actually watches. The manual labelling sample for FR-15 should be planned at 150 to 200 posts with the exact n stated in Chapter 3, since labelling all 730 before defence is unrealistic. Chapter 1's requirements table still specifies four roles and must be corrected to three. FR-25 through FR-30 are not yet named in the Objectives of the Study and should either be folded under Objectives 3 and 4 explicitly or flagged to the adviser as a deliberate breadth decision. And the disposition of the cut-feature code is a housekeeping call to make once the rebuild is underway.
